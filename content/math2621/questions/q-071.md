### Prompt
In this question, all square roots are the principal branch.
(a) Where is $\sqrt{z + 1}$ holomorphic?
(b) Show that $\sqrt{z + 1} \sqrt{z - 1} = -\sqrt{z^2 - 1}$ when $\operatorname{Re} z < -1$.
(c) Show that $\sqrt{z^2 - 1}$ is holomorphic when $\operatorname{Re} z < -1$.
(d) Show that $\sqrt{z + 1} \sqrt{z - 1}$ is holomorphic on the complement of $[-1, 1]$.

### Official Answer / Solution Notes
(a) Holomorphic on $\mathbb{C} \setminus (-\infty, -1]$. (d) When crossing $(-\infty, -1)$, both square roots change sign simultaneously, so their product is continuous and analytic across $(-\infty, -1)$, leaving only the branch cut on $[-1, 1]$!
