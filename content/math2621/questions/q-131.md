### Prompt
Show that a bijective conformal mapping defined on the whole complex plane $\mathbb{C}$ is affine ($f(z) = az + b$), and that a bijective conformal mapping defined on $\mathbb{C} \setminus \{p\}$, where $p \in \mathbb{C}$, is a fractional linear transformation.
Hint: By composing $f$ with an affine mapping, you can suppose that $f(0) = 0$ and $f'(0) = 1$. Try to show that $z \mapsto (1/z) - (1/f(z))$ is holomorphic, bounded, and vanishes at $\infty$, hence is 0.

### Official Answer / Solution Notes
Since $f$ is injective, $\infty$ is an isolated singularity of $f$. By Casorati-Weierstrass, it cannot be essential (otherwise the image of a neighborhood of $\infty$ would be dense and violate injectivity). So $\infty$ is a pole. An entire function with a pole at $\infty$ must be a polynomial. Being bijective, its degree must be 1, so $f(z) = az+b$.
