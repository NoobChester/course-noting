### Prompt
Show that the function $f(z) = \frac{1}{\sin(1/z)}$ does not have a Laurent series about $0$ that converges to $f$ in $B^\circ(0, r)$ for any $r > 0$.

### Official Answer / Solution Notes
Zeros of $\sin(1/z)$ occur at $1/z = k\pi \implies z = \frac{1}{k\pi}$ for $k \in \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$. These poles accumulate at $z = 0$. Since $z = 0$ is a non-isolated singularity, there is no punctured disk $B^\circ(0, r)$ free of other singularities, so no Laurent series exists.
