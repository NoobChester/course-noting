### Prompt
Suppose that $f(z) = az^2 + bz + c$, where $a, b, c \in \mathbb{C}$ and $a \ne 0$.
(a) Show that the image of a straight line $\ell$ in $\mathbb{C}$ under $f$ either lies in a straight line or is a parabola.
(b) Show that the image of a straight line $\ell$ in $\mathbb{C}$ under $f$ lies in a straight line if and only if $\ell$ passes through $-b/2a$.

### Official Answer / Solution Notes
Completing the square: $f(z) = a(z + b/2a)^2 + (c - b^2/4a)$. Let $w = z + b/2a$. Then $\ell$ maps to a line in $w$. If the line in $w$ passes through $0$, its square is a ray (straight line). If it does not pass through $0$, $(x_0 + it)^2 = x_0^2 - t^2 + 2i x_0 t$, which is a parabola $u = x_0^2 - (v/2x_0)^2$.
