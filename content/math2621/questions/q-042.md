### Prompt
Suppose that
$$f(z) = \begin{cases} e^{-z^{-4}} & \text{when } z \ne 0 \\ 0 & \text{when } z = 0 \end{cases}$$
Show that the Cauchy–Riemann equations hold in $\mathbb{C}$. Is $f$ entire?
Hint: You do not need to compute the partial derivatives, except at 0.

### Official Answer / Solution Notes
Answer: $f$ is holomorphic in $\mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}$, so CR equations hold there. At $0$, along the axes $z = x$ and $z = iy$, $e^{-x^{-4}} \to 0$ exponentially fast, so all partial derivatives at $0$ are $0$, satisfying CR. However, along the ray $z = r e^{i\pi/4}$, $z^4 = -r^4$, so $f(z) = e^{1/r^4} \to \infty$ as $r \to 0$. Hence $f$ is not continuous at $0$, and thus not entire!
