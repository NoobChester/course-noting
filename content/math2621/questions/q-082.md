### Prompt
Define the semicircular arc $\gamma_R$ by $\gamma_R(t) = R e^{it}$, where $0 \le t \le \pi$ and $R > 1$ is a real constant. Let $\gamma$ be the join of $\gamma_R$ and the line segment from $-R$ to $R$.
(a) Show that, if $z \in \operatorname{Range}(\gamma_R)$, then $\left|\frac{e^{iz}}{z^2+1}\right| \le \frac{1}{R^2-1}$.
(b) Deduce that $\left|\int_{\gamma_R} \frac{e^{iz}}{z^2+1} dz\right| \le \frac{\pi R}{R^2-1}$.
(c) Evaluate $\int_\gamma \frac{e^{iz}}{z^2+1} dz$.
(d) Hence find $\int_0^\infty \frac{\cos x}{x^2+1} dx$.

### Official Answer / Solution Notes
(c) Only singularity inside upper semicircle is $z = i$. By CIF, $\int_\gamma \frac{e^{iz}/(z+i)}{z-i} dz = 2\pi i \frac{e^{-1}}{2i} = \pi e^{-1}$. (d) As $R \to \infty$, arc integral vanishes, giving $\int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{\cos x}{x^2+1} dx = \pi/e$, so $\int_0^\infty = \frac{\pi}{2e}$.
