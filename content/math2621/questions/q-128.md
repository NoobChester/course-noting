### Prompt
Use the Laplace transform to solve the differential equation
$$\frac{d^2u}{dt^2} + 4\frac{du}{dt} + 3u = e^{-2t},$$
where $u : [0,\infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfies the initial conditions $u(0) = 0$ and $\frac{du}{dt}(0) = 0$.

### Official Answer / Solution Notes
$(z^2 + 4z + 3)U(z) = \frac{1}{z+2} \implies U(z) = \frac{1}{(z+1)(z+2)(z+3)} = \frac{1/2}{z+1} - \frac{1}{z+2} + \frac{1/2}{z+3}$. Thus $u(t) = \frac{1}{2}e^{-t} - e^{-2t} + \frac{1}{2}e^{-3t}$.
