### Prompt
(Not examinable) Suppose that $a \in \mathbb{R}$. Use the polar form of the Cauchy–Riemann equations to find where the function $f(z) = |z|^a e^{ia \operatorname{Arg}(z)}$ is differentiable.

### Official Answer / Solution Notes
Writing $z = r e^{i\theta}$, $f(z) = r^a e^{ia\theta} = r^a \cos(a\theta) + i r^a \sin(a\theta)$. $u_r = a r^{a-1} \cos(a\theta) = \frac{1}{r} v_\theta$, and $v_r = a r^{a-1} \sin(a\theta) = -\frac{1}{r} u_\theta$. The polar CR equations are satisfied everywhere away from the branch cut $\theta = \pi$ and $r = 0$.
