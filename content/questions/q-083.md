### Prompt
Let $f(z)$ be an entire function that satisfies the inequality $|f(z)| \le 1 + |z|$ for all $z \in \mathbb{C}$. Show that $f(z) = az + b$ for fixed complex numbers $a$ and $b$.
Hint: Apply the generalized Cauchy integral formula on an arbitrarily large circle to show that $f^{(n)}(0) = 0$ when $n \ge 2$.

### Official Answer / Solution Notes
By Cauchy's estimate, $|f''(z_0)| \le \frac{2! \max_{|z-z_0|=R}|f(z)|}{R^2} \le \frac{2(1 + |z_0| + R)}{R^2} \to 0$ as $R \to \infty$. Thus $f''(z) = 0$ for all $z \in \mathbb{C}$, which implies $f'(z) = a$ and $f(z) = az + b$.
