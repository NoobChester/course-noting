### Prompt
Suppose that $f$ is a nonconstant rational function. What can you say about the range of $f$? Hint: Write $f$ as $p/q$ and try to solve $p(z)/q(z) = \lambda$; this is possible if and only if $\lambda$ is in the range of $f$.

### Official Answer / Solution Notes
By the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, $p(z) - \lambda q(z) = 0$ has a complex root for every $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$, unless $p(z) - \lambda q(z)$ reduces to a non-zero constant. Thus, the range of a nonconstant rational function is either the entire complex plane $\mathbb{C}$, or $\mathbb{C}$ minus at most one single value (e.g. $f(z) = 1/z$ omits $0$).
