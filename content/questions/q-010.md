### Prompt
Explain both geometrically and algebraically why $|z + w| = |z| + |w|$ if $\arg z = \arg w$. Does the result extend to arbitrary finite sums of complex numbers?

### Official Answer / Solution Notes
If $\arg z = \arg w = \theta$, then $z = |z|e^{i\theta}$ and $w = |w|e^{i\theta}$, so $z+w = (|z|+|w|)e^{i\theta}$, hence $|z+w| = |z|+|w|$. Geometrically, the vectors point in the same direction, so the third side of the degenerate triangle equals the sum of the other two. By induction, yes, this extends to arbitrary finite sums with equal arguments.
