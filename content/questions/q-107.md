### Prompt
Find $I_1 = \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{\cos 6\theta}{5 + 4 \sin \theta} \, d\theta$ and $I_2 = \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{\sin 6\theta}{5 + 4 \sin \theta} \, d\theta$, by first computing $I_1 + iI_2$.

### Official Answer / Solution Notes
$I_1 + i I_2 = \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{e^{6i\theta}}{5 + 4 \sin\theta} d\theta = \oint_{|z|=1} \frac{z^6}{5 + 2(z - z^{-1})/i} \frac{dz}{iz} = \oint_{|z|=1} \frac{z^6}{2z^2 + 5iz - 2} dz$. Poles at $z = -i/2$ and $z = -2i$. Residue at $z = -i/2$ yields the exact value.
