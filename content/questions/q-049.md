### Prompt
In General Topology, a set $S$ is said to be connected if we cannot find two open sets $\Omega_1$ and $\Omega_2$ such that $\Omega_1 \cap \Omega_2 \cap S = \emptyset$, $\Omega_1 \cap S \ne \emptyset$, $\Omega_2 \cap S \ne \emptyset$ and $(\Omega_1 \cap S) \cup (\Omega_2 \cap S) = S$. Show that an open set $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ is connected in this sense if and only if it is arcwise connected. What can you say about regions?

### Official Answer / Solution Notes
For open subsets of $\mathbb{C}$, path-connectedness and topological connectedness are completely equivalent, because open disks are path-connected. Regions are connected open sets (with possibly boundary points), and domains are always polygonally path-connected.
