### Prompt
By integrating $f(z) = e^{-z^2}$ round the boundary of the rectangle with corners at $a, a + ib, -a + ib$ and $-a$, where $b > 0$, show that
$$\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-x^2} \cos 2bx \, dx = e^{-b^2} \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-x^2} \, dx = e^{-b^2}\sqrt{\pi}.$$

### Official Answer / Solution Notes
$f(z) = e^{-z^2}$ is entire, so by Cauchy's Theorem the closed rectangle integral is $0$. The vertical sides vanish as $a \to \infty$ since $|e^{-(a+iy)^2}| = e^{-a^2+y^2} \le e^{-a^2+b^2} \to 0$. The top edge gives $\int_a^{-a} e^{-(x+ib)^2} dx = -e^{b^2} \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-x^2}(\cos 2bx - i\sin 2bx) dx$.
