# Question 2: Circulation Verification via Green's Theorem

Let $C$ denote the unit circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ traversed counterclockwise, and let $\mathbf{F}$ be the vector field:
$$\mathbf{F}(x, y) = \begin{pmatrix} -y \\ x \end{pmatrix}$$

1. Evaluate the line integral $\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$ directly by parameterizing the curve $C$.
2. Apply Green's Theorem to evaluate the line integral as a double integral over the unit disk $D = \{(x, y) : x^2 + y^2 \le 1\}$.
3. Confirm that both results agree.

## Solution

### Part 1: Direct Line Integral Computation
Parameterize $C$ in polar coordinates:
$$\mathbf{r}(t) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos t \\ \sin t \end{pmatrix}, \quad t \in [0, 2\pi]$$

The velocity tangent vector is:
$$d\mathbf{r} = \begin{pmatrix} -\sin t \\ \cos t \end{pmatrix} dt$$

Evaluating $\mathbf{F}$ along the curve:
$$\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t)) = \begin{pmatrix} -\sin t \\ \cos t \end{pmatrix}$$

The line integral becomes:
$$\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \int_0^{2\pi} \begin{pmatrix} -\sin t \\ \cos t \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} -\sin t \\ \cos t \end{pmatrix} dt = \int_0^{2\pi} (\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t) dt = \int_0^{2\pi} 1\,dt = 2\pi$$

---

### Part 2: Green's Theorem Evaluation
Here $L(x, y) = -y$ and $M(x, y) = x$. We calculate partial derivatives:
$$\frac{\partial M}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x) = 1$$
$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(-y) = -1$$

Thus the 2D curl integrand is:
$$\frac{\partial M}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial L}{\partial y} = 1 - (-1) = 2$$

Applying Green's Theorem:
$$\oint_C (L\,dx + M\,dy) = \iint_D 2\,dA = 2 \iint_D dA = 2 \cdot \operatorname{Area}(D)$$

Since $D$ is the unit disk with radius $R = 1$, its area is $\pi (1)^2 = \pi$. Therefore:
$$\iint_D 2\,dA = 2\pi$$

---

### Part 3: Conclusion
Both the direct contour integration and the planar surface area integral yield $2\pi$, confirming Green's Theorem.
